A **transducer** (or **sensor**) is a device that converts one form of energy into another — most commonly transforming a physical quantity such as temperature, pressure, light, or motion into an electrical signal that can be measured, monitored, or processed by an [electronic system](../data-aquisition).

## Measurement error

Measurement error is the difference between the value reported by a transducer and the true value of the physical quantity being measured. It may be caused by calibration issues, offset, sensitivity changes, nonlinearity, hysteresis, drift, or environmental effects such as temperature and vibration.

Two important terms are **accuracy** and **precision**:

- **Accuracy** means how close a measurement is to the true value.
- **Precision** means how close repeated measurements are to one another.

A sensor can be precise without being accurate if its readings are consistently offset from the true value. Likewise, a sensor can be accurate on average but not precise if its readings vary widely.
